Quantum mechanics is the fundamental physical theory describing the behavior of matter and energy at atomic and subatomic scales. In quantum systems, physical quantities such as angular momentum, energy, and orbital state are restricted to discrete values, a phenomenon known as quantization.
Wave-particle duality asserts that every quantum entity exhibits both wave-like and particle-like characteristics. The mathematical foundation relies on state vectors in complex Hilbert space, evolving under unitary time transformation described by the Schrodinger equation.
The superposition principle allows quantum states to exist as linear combinations of orthogonal eigenstates simultaneously. When a measurement occurs, the wave function collapses into a definitive eigenstate with probability given by the Born rule. Quantum entanglement describes composite systems whose state cannot be factored into product states of individual components, demonstrating non-local correlations verified by Bell inequality experiments.
Geometric quantum mechanics formulates these phenomena using the geometry of projective Hilbert space, including the Fubini-Study metric and the Berry geometric phase. The S3 Hopf fibration maps three-dimensional spheres to two-dimensional spheres, providing a natural topological representation of two-level qubit systems and spinor rotations.
